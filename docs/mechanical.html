Mechanical and thermal

Housing, 75.0 mm baseline, RV1126B thermal path.

The demonstration tool (flash, live page, calibration) is in English.

The two faces

Where the heat is

Thermal path: RV1126B, centre of the camera side. Dual ISP, stereo and NPU. Cameras, eMMC and VS6621 stay clear of that path.

Cooling is part of the housing

The metal that holds the cameras is the heat path off the RV1126B.

1. Pick up the lid A 1–2 mm silicone gap pad (electrically isolating, 3 W/m·K or better) on the RV1126B lid, compressed 10–30 % onto a flat aluminium or steel wall. That wall can be the rear of the housing or a small spreader glued to it.
2. Use the via field A second pad on the connector-side via field, into the same metal.
3. One stiff volume The stereo baseline is the PCB. Housing holds the board, not the camera barrels. Bosses or a pocket under the green outline; cameras float in their holes.
4. Leave service faces USB-C, BOOT, RST and PH stay reachable, or come out on a short harness. Maskrom: hold BOOT, tap RST.

Mass and air surface

Passive. Lid pad onto aluminium that can see room air. No fan.

About 2.0 W with dual ISP and stereo, NPU off. About 3.0 W with the NPU on. Room 25 °C. Design die: 42 °C off, 45 °C on. Pad about 1.5 °C/W. Still aluminium in free air about 12 W/m²·K with radiation.

A = Psink / (h · (Tdie − 25 − Psink · 1.5)). About 0.4 W stays in the board, so the sink sees 1.6 W off and 2.6 W on.

Fins count as both faces. A 50 × 50 × 12 mm extrusion is about 100 cm². Pad the block to the RV1126B square. Fins see open air or sit on a thin aluminium housing wall.

At 30 °C room, stay at the top of the 45 °C band or add a few millimetres of fin height.

NPU off 50 × 50 × 12 mm, about 60 g. Dual ISP and depth stay in the low to mid 35–45 °C band in still air.
NPU on 60 × 60 × 16 mm, about 100 g. Same band, near 45 °C. A metal cover that size over the centre of the board is enough if the outside can breathe.

Assembly order

1

Board

  1. Handle the green outline, not the camera barrels and not the SoC lid.
  2. Both modules square. Baseline is this PCB.
  3. Keep the connector side clean. Flux or grease on the via field blocks the pad.
2

Thermal parts

  1. Lid pad on the RV1126B square only.
  2. Cut the back pad to the via field only. Stay off USB-C, PH, RST, BOOT and the UART pads.
  3. Stick pads to the metal first if that keeps them from sliding when the board goes in.
3

Housing

  1. Lower the board onto the bosses. Cameras enter their holes last, with no side load.
  2. Close the lid so the SoC pad compresses. Check that BOOT and RST still click.
  3. Route USB-C and PH without pulling the board against the thermal pads.
4

Prove the path

  1. Power from USB-C. Wait until stereo and ISP are up, then open the page with python tools/cv_ego_page.py.
  2. On-die sensor after depth is up: 35–45 °C (35000–45000). NPU off at the low end; NPU on near 45 °C.
  3. Above 45 °C: pad off the lid, or fins with no air.

Read the SoC temperature

On a board with ADB, the die sensor is the thermal zone:

adb shell cat /sys/class/thermal/thermal_zone0/temp

Millidegrees C. 40000 is 40 °C. Operating 35000–45000. Critical trip 115000 (115 °C). Zone follows cpu-thermal.
